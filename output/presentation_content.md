## 2. Motivation

- **Message:** diffusion erases fine detail, and the backward problem asks to recover it.

## 3. Mathematical background

- **Content:**
  - the equation u_t − Δu = f on Ω = (0,π)², t ∈ (0,T);
  - a glossary of every symbol;
  - the diffusivity is normalised to 1, as in the paper;
  - zero Dirichlet boundary conditions.
- **Forward vs. backward:**
  - forward: given u(·,0), find u(·,t);
  - backward: given u(·,T) = g, find u(·,t) for t < T.
- **Source:** eq. (1), p. 1.

## 4. Problem formulation

- **System (1):** u_t − u_xx − u_yy = f, homogeneous Dirichlet conditions, u(x,y,T) = g.
- **Known:**
  - g ∈ L²(Ω) and f ∈ L²(0,T;L²(Ω));
  - both are given inexactly, with ‖g_ε − g‖ ≤ ε and ‖f_ε − f‖ ≤ ε (p. 6).
- **Unknown:** u(·,t) for 0 ≤ t < T, in particular u(·,0).
- **Uniqueness:** Theorem 2, p. 6.

## 5. Why the backward problem is ill-posed

- **Eigen-structure:** eigenfunctions sin(mx) sin(ny), with λ_mn = m² + n² (eq. 4, p. 3).
- **Mode-wise solution (6):** u_mn(t) = e^{(T−t)λ} g_mn − ∫_t^T e^{(s−t)λ} f_mn ds.
- **Mechanism:**
  - a data error δ becomes δ e^{(T−t)λ};
  - this is unbounded as λ → ∞;
  - so Hadamard's third condition (continuous dependence on the data) fails.
  - The paper itself says "the term e^{(T−t)(m²+n²)} is the unstability's cause" (p. 3).
- **Bar chart:**
  - shows e^{5k²} for the benchmark modes (2k, k);
  - computed directly, e.g. e^5 ≈ 148, e^20 ≈ 4.9·10⁸, e^80 ≈ 5.5·10³⁴.
- **Note:** float64 rounding is blown up once modes with λ ≳ 40 are kept. This is from Experiment 2 (OURS).

## 6. The paper's method: Fourier truncation

- **Quote:** "The essence of our regularization method is just to eliminate all high frequencies from the solution" (p. 3).
- **Method:**
  - keep the modes with m² + n² ≤ M_ε;
  - invert those modes exactly with eq. (6);
  - M_ε → ∞ as ε → 0.
- **Theorem 3 (pp. 6–7):** ‖u(t) − v(t)‖² ≤ e^{2(T−t)M_ε}(‖g¹ − g²‖² + ‖f¹ − f²‖²).
- **Lattice plot:**
  - shows the modes kept for M³_ε = 13.87 at ε = 10⁻⁵;
  - the benchmark modes are circled;
  - only (2,1) is inside the disc.

## 7. Mathematical derivation (6 steps)

1. Fourier sine series with h_mn = (4/π²) ∫∫ h sin(mx) sin(ny) (p. 4).
2. Mode ODE: u_mn' + λ u_mn = f_mn, with u_mn(T) = g_mn.
3. Backward Duhamel formula (6).
4. Instability: e^{(T−t)λ} amplification, and the existence condition (8) from Theorem 1.
5. Truncate to λ ≤ M_ε.
6. Regularized solution (12), which is eq. (6) applied to g_ε and f_ε.

**Source:** pp. 2–6. Eq. (3) in the paper integrates over (0, T). That is a typo; (6) and (12) use (t, T).

## 13. Limitations and observations

**In the paper** (see `docs/paper_analysis.md`, A1–A13):
- three details are unstated: r, the form of M⁴, and the error metric;
- the constants in Theorems 4/5 are too small: the printed bound is 1.28 at ε = 10⁻³ but the actual error is 1.634 (A12);
- Theorem 6 has an exponent-sign issue (A13);
- "bigger M is better" holds only for M < 20 (A10);
- typos: the integration interval in eq. (3) (A4) and the Table 2 cell (A6).

**In our reproduction:**
- the noise realisation is unknown;
- the figures can only be compared visually;
- there is a single, special benchmark;
- noise in f is not exercised;
- noise-free data still break down for M ≳ 40 because of float64 rounding (relative error 2·10¹⁷ at M = 80).

## 14. Conclusion

Five takeaways:
1. The problem is ill-posed.
2. High frequencies are the reason.
3. Truncation caps the amplification (Theorem 3).
4. The reproduction succeeded.
5. Choosing M_ε is a trade-off. Only modes with λ ≲ ln(1/ε) are recoverable at t = 0.
